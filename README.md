# swift-particles

Use this package when a picture holds many particles: a galaxy of a hundred thousand stars, dust that gathers into a word and settles on a mesh, sparks streaking past, confetti thrown into the air. Start from `Particles.Population`, a seed and a count. Each particle reads its own draws (`Particles.Draws`), takes a home in a shape (`Particles.Spiral`, `Disc`, `Box`, `Points` with the letters of a `Picture.Text`, `Surface` of a mesh, `Trace` of a path), moves by a motion that gives its place at any instant (`Particles.Orbit`, `Passage`, `Flight` with a `Flutter`), is seen through a `Picture.Scene.Camera` (`Particles.View`), and is drawn as light (`Particles.Mote` added up in a `Particles.Light`) or as paper (`Particles.Card` laid in `Particles.Cards`, lit by a `Particles.Sheen`). Both hand the renderer one image as one `Picture.Element`. A particle has no state and nothing is stepped: any particle at any instant can be drawn alone, in any order, on any thread. The package draws through swift-picture and depends on swift-ballistics for flight and on swift-timeline for easing; it has no renderer of its own.

## Laws

- A particle is its ordinal in a population. Its draws are `Threefry.block` of swift-threefry, keyed by the population's seed, with the particle and the stream times 2³² plus half the index as counter, its first word for an even index and its second for an odd one, the top 53 bits over 2⁵³: in 0 up to 1, the same on every platform, and dependent only on the seed, the particle, the stream and the index. Streams below 2³² and indices below 2³³ draw distinct numbers. Give each use of a particle (its home, its look, its order in a passage) a stream of its own, so that changing one never moves another.
- A `Particles.Shape` gives a `Particles.Home` (a place, and the shape's unit normal there where it has one) as a function of draws alone, and says which draws it reads. A spiral's homes lie between its inner radius and its reach, within half its thickness of z = 0, and, unless they stray, within `width` across an arm, the arms turned by `turn(at:)`, with normal +z. A spiral's home is the place of its polar form (`polar(_:)`, a `Spiral.Polar`: distance, arm, angle aside from the arm's line in −π…π, height) at the angle `direction(of: arm) + (winding + wound) × share + aside + turned`, share the way from the inner radius to the reach; so `place(of:turned:wound:)` turns a particle as one body (a whole turn brings it back) and winds it tighter, by nothing at the inner radius and by all of `wound` at the reach, while it stays on its arm. A turn that differs with the distance, such as an `Orbit`'s, is passed as `turned`. A disc's lie in its plane within its radius, evenly by area; a box's within its extent, evenly by volume, without a normal. `Points` picks one of its places, each equally; from a text it holds `Picture.Text.points(spacing:seed:)`, which all lie inside the glyphs. A `Surface` places a home on a mesh by `Mesh.Sampler` of swift-mesh, evenly by area, with the vertex normals interpolated there at unit length. A `Trace` places a home on a line, evenly by length: a path flattened to within a tolerance, or a chain of places in order (`init(through:closed:gap:)`) whose steps longer than `gap` are breaks. A particle on a line is its distance along it, and `place(along:)` takes a distance around the whole length, so a home whose distance grows with time flows along the line and round again, leaping across each break between pieces at once; `unbroken(from:to:)` says whether a stretch crosses a break, so a streak is not drawn across one. Shapes drawn from picture space (`Points` of a text, `Trace`) lie in the world's plane z = 0 with picture y turned up and the middle of their box at the origin.
- A motion gives a place at an instant (a `Rational` number of seconds) and nothing else. `Orbit` turns a place about z by `rate × t × (radius / r)^shear` at a distance r from the axis, so a shear winds what it turns, and is the place itself at 0. `Passage` moves a particle of an order in 0…1 from one place to another: it leaves at `start + (end − each − start) × order`, takes `each`, and goes the share its `Timeline.Easing` gives, so order 0 leaves at the start and order 1 arrives at the end; before it leaves it is exactly at the first place and after it arrives exactly at the second. With a monotone easing and no turn it only ever comes nearer; a `turn` bends the way about an axis by `turn × 4s(1 − s)` at the share s, which leaves both ends and the distance to the end unchanged. `Flight` is a throw: along each axis a `Ballistics.Trajectory` with one drag, at its origin until its launch (the particle's own time in seconds, a `Double`: what is exact is the instant a picture is taken at, not each particle's stagger), plus a sway along x that grows as drag settles it; `pose(at:)` adds a spin about the piece's normal and a tumble about its width, two perpendicular unit axes at every instant.
- `Particles.View` sees a place through a camera of swift-picture. Its position, normalised depth and distance are the camera's `sight(of:in:viewport:)`, bit for bit, made from the camera's products once; a place in front of the near plane is not seen. `scale` is how many picture units a unit of the world across the line of sight spans there, so a mote of a size in the world shrinks and dims with distance as a light does. A `Particles.Focus` blurs a place at a distance d by `aperture × |d − distance| / d`, the thin lens's circle of confusion.
- A `Mote`'s whole light is its colour's pixel times its strength times `π × radius² / 3`, what a soft `Picture.Sprites.Sprite` of that colour and radius adds. `Light` spreads it over a soft disc of radius `√(radius² + blur²)`, at least `footprint` pixels, by the weights `(1 − d²/R²)²` at the pixel centres divided by their sum, so the raster gains exactly the mote's whole light wherever it falls between pixel centres: a particle that moves by less than a pixel does not flicker or dim, a blur spreads light without changing it, and a sharp mote is about its colour at its centre.
- `add(trail:)` draws a particle read at evenly spaced instants across a shutter (`Particles.Shutter.instants(closing:)`): each straight piece between two motes takes an equal share of the motes' mean light, spread by the soft disc swept along the piece (the integral along it in closed form, again divided by the weights' sum). So a streak's light is the still particle's, a long streak shows no dashes, and a trail of equal motes is that mote: a still particle draws as a point. That is a shutter's law, under which a long streak is dim; `add(trail:persistence:)` multiplies the trail's light by `g^persistence`, g = `L / 2F(min(L/2, R))` how much a streak of length L dims the middle of its way below a still mote's centre (F the profile's integral, R the mean spread in pixels, so g is 1 when still and `15L / 16R` beyond the disc): 1 keeps the brightness along the way instead of the light.
- Light adds, so the order of motes does not matter up to rounding and nothing is sorted. `element` is the light's rectangle filled with its raster as an image in a layer under `lighter`; colour above 1 is kept for a bloom or threshold to find, and alpha is held to 1. The same motes give the same raster, and the renderer draws it the same on every thread count.
- `Mote.mixed(to:at:through:)` is a mote part of the way to another, the share held to 0…1: position along the straight line or the way a `Passage` bends, radius, strength and blur `a(1 − s) + bs`, colour mixed as written in sRGB; at 0 it is the first and at 1 the second, exactly.
- A `Card` is a flat piece seen: the parallelogram its two projected half-axes span, or the ellipse inside it, of a colour times a brightness `1 − shade × (1 − f)` for f how squarely it faces the eye from either side. Under a `Particles.Sheen`, a light far away, it also glints: `strength × |n · h|^sharpness` of the way from that lit paper to the light's colour, n its normal and h the half-way direction between the light and the eye, so a tumbling piece flashes as it turns through that direction. `Cards` lays cards far to near by distance, in the order added at equal distances, each over what is below. A card covers a pixel by the card filtered by a box 2h wide, h its blur in pixels and at least half a pixel: for the rectangle the product over its two pairs of sides of the share of that box between them, so the whole area is kept however wide the blur and a piece seen edge-on thins to nothing; for the ellipse the same smoothing across its edge. A card is drawn within its `extent(scale:)`: the box of its parallelogram widened by twice its softness (its blur, at least half a pixel), which holds the whole blurred card unless its axes meet at less than 60°; `reach(scale:)` is the radius of the disc around its centre that holds the extent. `faded(by:)` fades its paper and glint alike.
- A `Clearance` keeps a rectangle readable: the opacity of something of an extent is its `floor` where it reaches the rectangle, 1 from `feather` beyond, and `3u² − 2u³` of the share u of the feather between; among several, the least. Multiply a mote's strength by it, or fade a card by it (`faded(by:)` on either), giving a card its `reach` as the extent.
- Homes are functions of draws; a mesh home is a search over the mesh's triangles and allocates, a text's lattice is made once by `Points`. A consumer that draws many frames keeps its population's homes in an array, made once (100 000 homes in three shapes: about 0.45 s).
- Motions take a `Rational` instant and also seconds (`after:`), and `Shutter.seconds(closing:)` makes a shutter's instants seconds once, since a `Rational` is exact and slow to read for every particle; for the same reason a `Flight`'s launch is a `Double`: 90 000 flights are built in about 0.02 s and their launches read in about 1 ms a frame, against 0.1 s and 30 ms when each launch was a `Rational` (release build, the machine shared, so the times are rough).
- Spreading a sharp mote costs about the pixels of its disc twice, a streak about the pixels of its swept disc twice; trail pieces shorter together than a quarter of a pixel are spread as one.
- On an Apple silicon Mac with eight cores (arm64, macOS 27.2, release build, measured as processor time on a loaded machine), a full-HD frame of 100 000 particles on one thread takes: a still galaxy turning (`Spiral` homes kept, `Orbit`, `View`, `Mote`, `Light`) 0.31 s; the same through a shutter read at six instants 0.68 s; the population in the middle of a passage from the galaxy to a word, streaking, 1.1 s. The renderer draws the light, one image, in about 0.18 s of processor time, 0.03 s on eight threads. 1800 confetti cards with depth of field are laid in about 0.01 s.
- To use more cores, spread parts of the population into lights of their own on threads and `add` them in a fixed order: the raster is then the same for any number of threads.
- The `Threads` trait turns on swift-picture's `Threads`, so the tests draw through `Picture CPU` on several threads (`swift test --traits Threads`); without it they draw on one.
